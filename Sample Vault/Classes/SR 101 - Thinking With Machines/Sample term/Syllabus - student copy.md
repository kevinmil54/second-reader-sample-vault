# SR 101: Thinking With Machines

*A sample six-week course that comes with the Second Reader starter vault. Use it to try the workflow on real readings, or replace it with your own course.*

## About the course

Large language models now sit beside people while they read, write, study and make decisions. This course looks at what the research says happens to the people. We start with what people actually use AI assistants for, then look at effort and confidence, learning, writing and memory, creativity and companionship, and finally at who is making the decisions when an agent does the work.

The readings are recent and mostly empirical. Several are preprints, so part of the job is judging how much weight a single study can carry.

## How this vault works

Each reading below has two links.

- **open pdf** opens your own copy of the paper in Zotero's PDF reader. It works once you have made that reading's literature note.
- **Literature note** is your note on the reading. Its color shows where you are: dimmed until you make it, normal once it exists, yellow once you've synced Zotero highlights into it, green once you've written in it.

To make a literature note, run **Second Reader: Literature Note** from the command palette (Cmd/Ctrl+P) and pick the reading. Read in Zotero with the note open beside you, click the highlighter button at the top right of the note to bring your highlights in with page numbers, and write your own notes as you go.

When an idea is worth keeping on its own, write it as a candidate under "Permanent note candidates" and check the box. Second Reader makes a permanent note in `Permanent notes/` and turns the line into a link to it. Over the term, gather permanent notes on one question into a note in `Synthesis notes/` that says what you now think.

Each week's class notes go in `Meeting notes/`, one note per meeting.

## What you'll do

1. Literature notes for each week's readings, handed in with that week's **Package my Week N literature notes for upload** link. It writes a zip to `Uploads/` and shows it in Finder or Explorer.
2. At least two permanent notes a week, in your own words.
3. One synthesis note by the end of the course, on a question of your choice from the readings.

## On using GenAI in this course

You may use GenAI tools as a second reader: to question your summary, to point out what you skipped, or to argue with a permanent note. Don't use them to read for you or to write your notes. The literature-note template asks how you read each paper, and one of the options is "GenAI read this for me." Be honest there.
